Spring 2022

A class of freshmen arrived on campus having spent their entire first year on Zoom. There was no structured way to meet people, and no spontaneous one either. Everyone was a little out of practice at being around each other.

So we asked: what is the smallest thing that would make one stranger reach for another?

From cardboard to kiosk

01 / 06
01

The machine was me

The first prototype cost less than $10. I hid inside a cardboard vending machine and handed out quotes, riddles, life hacks, and quizzes to 50 people at a trade show.

quotes, keptriddles, returnedlife hacks, returnedquizzes, returned
02

What people kept

Quotes went into wallets and phone cases. Everything else was set back on the table. People held onto meaning and nothing else, and that became the brief.

03

A mirror you can carry

Laser-engraved mirrored acrylic: you see your own face before you read the prompt. Twenty of every card, all reusable, each with a QR code on the back.

04

A ritual, not an interface

We made a short film to teach the gesture: find a card, pass it on, enter the code, read your message.

05

So the message became a secret

Visible messages felt too exposed to hand to a stranger. The final card carries only a code. You write privately, the recipient prints the note, and the card is wiped clean.

06

The reflection machine

An iridescent kiosk where the note finally appears, printed on paper you can keep.

The prompts

Every card opened with the same invitation, and ended in one of four ways. Each was a small, specific reason to walk up to someone.

What the cards revealed

150 scans in the first week, from orchestra rehearsals to dining halls to dorm rooms.

5 people deep: the farthest a single card traveled across campus.

But vulnerability travels short distances. Tender, visible messages stayed between close friends. Secret notes reached strangers.

Printed, kept, passed on

The build

Four people, one cabinet, no kits

Raspberry PiThe brain: reads each card code and sends the message to print
Thermal printerTurns a stranger's words into paper you can keep
Microsoft SurfaceFirst generation, repurposed as the touchscreen
Hand-built cabinetWe drilled the cabinetry, wired the electronics, and wrote the code ourselves

Then we wheeled it into a freshman dorm, where 100 freshmen used it. Afterwards it stayed at the Stanford d.school as an installation for years.

Still curious

Every card left a trace. Together the traces drew a map of who reaches for whom, and which words travel farthest. I was offered a grant to keep going, and I still wonder what we could build if we could see that map for a whole city.
